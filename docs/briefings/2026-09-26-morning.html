What the data said

Three findings from real data

Finding 1. A current iPhone's tracking stays inside the server's allowance

Position error is the distance between where the phone thinks it is and where it really is, sideways drift included. We measured it after walks of 3 to 30 ft on two public datasets, with the same code for both (make drift and make modern-arkit in PR #12).

Scale error works like a tape printed too small

Every reading comes up short, and the longer the reading, the more inches you lose. On the 2018 phone, ARKit read distances 5% to 17% short against GPS. On the current iPhone, its scale matched the dataset's reference within 2% on 30 of 35 outdoor walks.

Where the picture breaks. A misprinted tape is off by the same amount every time, while the 2018 phone's shortfall changed from walk to walk. And a tape only measures along the walk; position error also counts sideways drift.

Two caveats on the current iPhone. MARViN doesn't say how its reference got its meters, so it may have been scaled to ARKit itself. And that phone has LiDAR, which the phones we target mostly lack.

What it means. The server widens every AR and photo measurement's error bar by 0.16 ft for each foot away from the meter, so a far-away measurement comes back "not sure" instead of passing wrongly. That allowance is right for current phones. A tape test on a current, non-LiDAR iPhone is still the only direct measurement.

Finding 2. Learned 3D from photos gets flat walls to about 5 in at best, never edges

We tested on ETH3D: real building scans with laser ground truth, points within 6 m of the camera, 1 to 3 m spans. Each model turns a photo into depth. On its own, each one gets the size of the scene wrong.

One taped distance fixes the typical case, not the bad ones

One photo plus one taped distance:

9.6 inp90 on surfaces
15.1 inp90 on walls
26.9 inp90 at edges

The best recipe: let the phone's poses set the scale

A pose is where the phone stood and which way it pointed for a photo. ARKit records one for every photo. We take MoGe-2's depth and rescale it with the phone's own poses over 8 photos.

Verdict. At a realistic phone error, learned depth gets flat walls to about 5 in p90, and only with perfect feature matching between photos. Edges (window and door frames, equipment) stay at 8 in or worse in every setting.

For the app. Measurements that decide a check should come from AR taps, which carry the phone's own tracking error from Finding 1, not from depth maps. Learned depth is useful for coverage and the 3D picture, not for clearances near a threshold.

Finding 3. Meter close-up: reading works, finding the number doesn't yet

The question: how often does Apple's on-device text recognition read the meter number and class from real, openly licensed meter photos (Wikimedia Commons)? And which cheap photo checks (blur, glare, size) predict a failed read?

We read 80 such photos in the accurate mode. The reference labels come from two AI readers, not a person yet.

  • Reading works. The full meter number came out right on 71 of 73 photos (97%). The US class label (CL200 and similar) read on 20 of 22. The fast mode reads only 75% of numbers, so the app uses the accurate mode.
  • Finding the number doesn't, yet. A nameplate carries serials, model codes and seal numbers too. "Take the tallest line of digits" picked the meter number on only 21 of 75 photos, and no text-only rule did better than 29. Barcodes help: one was decoded on 25 of the 80 photos, and it held the meter number on 20 of 24. No rule reached 95% precision on held-out photos, and a tap-to-confirm list of three contained the number on 85% of them, short of the 95% bar. Half the misses are plates with several real identifiers, so Base has to say which one it needs. Recommendation: always show the candidates and let the homeowner tap the right one, with a barcode match listed first.
  • When to ask for a retake. Two checks work from what the phone can see: blur (a sharpness score) and a number that is too small (its line 34 px tall or less, so "move closer"). Glare, framing and hand shake have no reliable signal yet, so those prompts can only be general advice.
  • Caveat. These are deliberate, well-lit close-ups. A homeowner's photos will be worse, so 97% is an upper bound, and the thresholds need checking on live camera frames on field day.

Decide this morning

Eight decisions, with the manager's recommendation

1Run the field test first today?

Recommend
Yes, first thing this morning, about an hour. Take a current iPhone to a real meter wall and run Measure Lab's one-hour tape protocol (PR #7) plus the field checklist in experiments/evals/README.md section 5 (PR #12): two long taped spans, one taped scale reference, a rig tap at every surveyed point. Follow experiments/evals/field/FIELD_SHEET.md (PR #12): it fixes the tap order so Measure Lab's M1-M10 match the pre-named templates, and you only type tape readings. make field SESSION=… TRUTH=… MAP=… then gives one scored table with the phone's scale error; a dry run on synthetic data recovered a planted 1.5% error exactly.
Why
This one number, the phone's scale error, decides how accurate the whole product can be.
If not
Every accuracy claim keeps resting on public data from a LiDAR iPhone, checked against a reference that may have been scaled to ARKit itself. A tape test on a current, non-LiDAR iPhone is the only direct measurement.

2Measure with AR taps, not depth maps?

Recommend
Every point a check depends on (window, door and wall edges, the meter, obstacles) is an AR tap the homeowner makes or confirms. Learned depth fills the 3D picture and coverage only.
Why
Depth edges were 8 in or worse in every test, and even flat walls only reached about 5 in p90 at a realistic phone error.
If not
A clearance check near its threshold passes or fails on depth-map error, which at edges is 8 in or worse.

3Walk both ends or stop early?

Recommend
Walk outward from the meter on both sides, and stop once the nearest spot that fits has all its evidence and everything closer has been seen. Reaching both wall ends is not required.
Why
The solver ranks spots by shortest cable run, then by distance from the meter, so once everything closer is seen on both sides, walking farther cannot find a better spot. A shorter walk also drifts less (Finding 1).
If not
Walking to both ends costs the homeowner time and adds far-away measurements the answer rarely needs. Stopping at the first spot that fits on one side can miss a closer spot on the other. Either way, unseen ground is never clear: the 05:40 review found one path in #11 where it could still pass; that is fixed and tested now.

4Load Base's real rules

Recommend
Put Base's actual values into private/rules.yaml (git-ignored; the server merges it over the public file), and confirm with Base which rules the demo follows and which identifier on the meter plate they need. Plates often carry several.
Why
With the public placeholder rules, automatic approval is off, so every real scan ends in "An installer will take a look", even when all checks pass.
If not
The demo can never show a real "fits here" answer.

5Merge order

Recommend
#11, #14, #15, #10, #13, #12, then #7, #4, #8, #6, #16.
Why
Server first, because the app, the web page and the docs PR's rules sentence depend on it.

6Who owns each lane A-D today?

Recommend
No recommendation. The team names an owner per lane.
Why
Agents need to know whose OK they need before touching a lane's files.
If not
Agent work on lane files waits for someone to answer.

7Who checks what's hidden behind things?

Recommend
The app owns it. On every phone, show the homeowner the photo of the chosen spot before the result and ask whether anything blocks the wall or ground there. On LiDAR phones, also run a depth test: against true depth it removed the hidden stretch cleanly (0.1 ft left, no good wall lost, when 0.4 m of wall relief is allowed). Requiring views from different angles doesn't work: it removed the hidden stretch only by also throwing away 6 to 8.5 ft of good wall.
Why
Run on real photos of a building with a laser scan, the app's coverage map claimed 1.1 ft of a 19.3 ft wall that no photo saw, hidden behind scaffold footings. The server never sees the photos, so it can't catch this, yet it treats everything the app reports as seen.
If not
A gas meter or vent hidden behind a bush could sit inside a clearance the server marks as clear.

8What must the scan see, and what can we just ask?

Recommend
The app measures how far out it really saw the ground and asks only as far as the nearest check needs. The path the homeowner walked counts as proof the space in front of the wall is clear, and one "tilt up" view at the chosen spot covers headroom. A corner the homeowner marks ends the wall instead of becoming a request. Rare, far-away hazards (a pool, a driveway) become one yes/no question, labelled as the homeowner's answer.
Why
The verification agent found that today no scan can finish on the first try, however well it's walked: the app always reports ground 4 ft out while the checks need about 6 to 13 ft, it never reports the space in front of or above the wall, and each corner becomes a "walk past this" request. On the real replay that left 8 checks unsure and 6 follow-up requests.
If not
Every homeowner gets asked for photos they can't take, the opposite of Sam's first priority.
