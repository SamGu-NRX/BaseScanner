1. The problem: a battery has to fit between the rules

Base Power puts a home battery on the outside wall, close to the electric meter, because a cable runs from the meter to the battery. The battery is 31 in wide, 22 in deep and 39.5 in tall. Whether it fits, and where, depends on the clearance rules in the picture. Today a person has to go to the house and check.

Our product does that check with the homeowner's iPhone, in a couple of minutes. The app measures the wall. A server applies the rules in plain code. The app shows where the battery goes, in AR, on the real wall.

The demo's rules come from public sources and live in server/rules.yaml, each with its citation. Two numbers there are placeholders because no public value exists (pool 10 ft, driveway 5 ft). Base's own thresholds are private, load at runtime from a file that is not in git, and do not appear on this page.

5. The main blocker, and what to do this morning

Run the field test first, about an hour. You need a current non-LiDAR iPhone, the Measure Lab build, a 30 ft tape and a real meter wall. Follow experiments/evals/field/FIELD_SHEET.md: type the tape readings into the prepared templates, and one command turns them into a score and the phone's real scale error. A dry run recovered a planted 1.5% error exactly, so the scoring end is ready.

The overnight tracking numbers (finding 1) come from a 2023 iPhone with LiDAR and a dataset whose reference may itself be scaled to the phone's tracking. Most homeowners' phones lack LiDAR. The field test measures our own phone on our own wall, which is the only direct measurement.

6. What the overnight tests found, and what each changed

All five ran on public real-world datasets with laser-measured ground truth. Dataset images are never in git. Each finding below ends with the line that answers "did this test do anything".

Finding 1. How far off is the phone's own tracking?

What it changed: the server's error allowance comes from this. The field test measures our own phone.

Finding 2. Can photos alone give 3D measurements?

What it changed: 3D modeling stays central. Learned depth alone is too rough at edges, so LiDAR and the phone's poses supply the scale. World models are not evaluated yet. (Corrected at midday; the 09:10 version said to drop photo-to-3D measurement.)

Finding 3. Does the app's coverage map claim wall it never saw?

What it changed: decision 7 (who checks what is hidden). A wrong claim in the app's code, "the server rechecks from the images", was corrected: the server never sees images.

Finding 4. Can the phone read the meter?

What it changed: the app shows three candidates to tap, barcode match first, plus two photo checks that trigger a retake (blurry; number too small, "move closer").

Finding 5. Can a first-time homeowner finish a scan?

What it changed: the capture contract, and the app fixes in section 4: the server publishes exactly what settles each check, and the app reports real ground depth, the walked path as clear space in front, and a tilt-up view for headroom.

Three pieces of overnight work changed nothing in the product: the panel-label search (too few open photos: 7 US panels against 40 needed), finer statistics on the modern-phone data, and the research notes.

7. Decisions for this morning, recommendation first

  1. Run the field test first

    Recommend: about an hour, before anything else.

    A current non-LiDAR iPhone, Measure Lab, a 30 ft tape, a real meter wall. Follow experiments/evals/field/FIELD_SHEET.md. One command turns tape readings into a score and the phone's real scale error.

  2. How points get measured

    Recommend: metric depth where precision decides a check.

    Take those points from LiDAR or AR taps; learned depth alone gets edges to 8 in or worse (Finding 2). The 3D model still carries everything else. Updated 11:40 after the team split.

  3. How far to walk

    Recommend: outward from the meter on both sides; stop when the nearest spot that fits is fully seen.

    The server prefers the shortest cable, so walking farther cannot find a better spot, and shorter walks drift less.

  4. Base's real rules

    Recommend: load them into the private rules file now.

    Confirm with Base which rules the demo follows and which number on the meter plate they need (plates carry several). Otherwise the demo can never show a real "fits here".

  5. Merge order

    Recommend: #11 server first, since the others depend on its schemas.

    Then #14 docs, #10 app, #13 verification, #12 evals, then #7, #4, #8, #6, #16, #17, #19.

  6. Lane owners

    Recommend: name one owner per lane.

    Capture app, geometry, rules, output. Agents then know whose OK they need.

  7. Who checks what is hidden behind things?

    Recommend: the app.

    Before the result, show the homeowner the photo of the chosen spot and ask if anything blocks the wall or ground there. On LiDAR phones, also run a depth test (finding 3).

  8. What must the scan see, and what can we just ask?

    Recommend: pool and driveway become one yes/no question, labelled as the homeowner's answer.

    Instead of requiring 8 to 13 ft of photographed ground.
