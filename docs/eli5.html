Where does the battery go? 🔋🏠

Some families get a big battery that sits outside, next to their house. It keeps the lights on when the power goes out. But it can't go just anywhere. Here's how our app finds the perfect spot.

THE PROBLEM

Photos can't measure 📸🤷

Right now, families send in photos. But a photo is flat! It can't tell whether the gap between two windows is as big as a bathtub or as small as a lunchbox. And if someone forgets to photograph part of the wall, nobody knows what's there.

For grown-ups: a single photo has no real scale, so distances get estimated by eye. Borderline cases end up with a human reviewer, and any wall nobody photographed turns into a "please send more photos" follow-up.

STEP 1

The magic measuring phone 📱✨

The family holds up their phone and walks along the wall, like playing "follow the wall." The phone counts how far it went, like counting footsteps. Fancy phones even shoot out invisible laser dots to feel how far things are, the way a bat uses sound! The phone says "keep going!" until it has seen the wall all the way to the end, both ways.

Wall seen

For grown-ups: ARKit tracks the phone in real feet using the camera plus the motion sensors. LiDAR iPhones add a 3D mesh (about ±6–8 cm on walls, out to about 5 m). The capture won't finish until both wall ends are tapped. Anything the phone still hasn't seen counts as unsure, never as clear.

STEP 2

A robot plays I Spy 🤖🔍

The pictures go to a helper computer. A robot looks at them and plays I Spy: "I spy a window! I spy a door! I spy the gas meter!" Then it sticks a label on each thing, in exactly the right spot along the wall.

For grown-ups: Gemini draws the boxes. It had the best zero-shot box accuracy in the research, and it returns boxes natively. SAM 2 tightens each box, and supervision merges duplicates across frames. Because the phone knows where it was for every photo, each box gets cast onto the wall and becomes "window, 2–5 ft along the wall, 3–6.5 ft up."

STEP 3

Looking down like a bird 🐦🗺️

Some things are easier to see from the sky: the driveway where cars go, the swimming pool, and which side of the house faces the street. So the helper also checks a map, like a bird looking down.

For grown-ups: Census geocoder → Overture building outline → osmnx nearest road → street-facing wall. Pools and driveways come from samgeo on public-domain StratMap/NAIP aerial photos. OSM tags them on under 5% of homes. Don't run ML on Google or Mapbox imagery; their terms forbid it.

STEP 4 · YOUR TURN

Slide the box and check the rulebook 🧩

Drag the battery along the wall. Every time it moves, the helper checks the rulebook. Green means that rule is happy. Red means "not here!" Then press Let the computer try every spot to watch it test every single position, one tiny step at a time.

    STEP 5

    Show and tell 👀✨

    The phone draws a pretend battery right on the real wall, like a sticker floating in the air, plus a purple line showing where the wire goes. The family looks through their phone and says, "Oh! That's where it goes!"

    For grown-ups: RealityKit places a box the battery's size (Base Core is 39.5″ × 30.68″ × 22″), attached to the meter's anchor rather than raw world coordinates, because ARKit corrects anchors as the phone moves. It's also the moment the family gets to say whether they like how it looks.

    WHY IT MATTERS

    Why this is better 🏆

    Instead of guessing from flat photos, the phone measures, makes sure it saw everything, and a fair rulebook picks the spot.

    📏

    Real feet, not guesses

    A flat photo can't tell 3 feet from 8 feet. The phone measures to within a few inches.

    🔁

    No "take more photos" loop

    The phone won't finish until it has seen every part the rules need, so families don't get asked for more pictures later.

    📖

    Every answer has a reason

    "Too close to the gas meter" comes from a real safety rule, not a robot's feeling. That makes it easy to trust and to double-check.
